# Discipline profiles for literature synthesis

Profiles supply default classification axes. The review question and user's natural-language priorities always take precedence.

## `general`

Classify by problem, approach, finding, explanation, contribution, robustness, limitation, and transferability.

## `medicine`

Compare population, design, eligibility, intervention or exposure, comparator, endpoints, effect measures, follow-up, confounding, bias, safety, clinical significance, external validity, registration, and ethics.

## `chemistry`

Compare chemical systems, routes, stoichiometry, solvents, operating conditions, yield, selectivity, characterization, kinetics, thermodynamics, mechanistic support, reproducibility, scale-up, safety, and environmental cost.

## `materials`

Compare composition, phase, structure, defects, interfaces, processing, characterization, test conditions, performance definitions, structure-property relations, stability, manufacturability, cost, and baselines.

## `catalysis`

Compare catalyst and active phase, sites, support, preparation, reaction system, reactor, illumination or electrochemical conditions, normalization, selectivity, durability, deactivation, kinetics, transport, adsorption, intermediates, controls, and operando support.

## `physics`

Compare physical regimes, assumptions, symmetries, models, equations, boundary conditions, setups, observables, error ranges, approximations, scale laws, limiting cases, validation, and parameter sensitivity.

## `mathematics`

Compare problem formulations, notation, assumptions, theorem strength, proof architecture, key lemmas, technical innovations, generality, special cases, counterexamples, constructiveness, complexity implications, and open problems.

Use secondary profiles only when they add a real comparison axis. Do not create an empty taxonomy from every listed dimension.
